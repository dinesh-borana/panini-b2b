# 01 — Product Thinking & Discovery Process

## Q: Walk me through how you'd start a new product or feature.

**Short answer:** Problem discovery first (what's broken, why now). Then user discovery (who, current alternatives, JTBD). Then strategy, success metrics, risks → one-page brief. Only after that: MVP scoping, user flows, technical design. Juniors start with features; seniors start with problems.

**Long answer:** Eight steps: (1) Problem discovery, (2) User discovery, (3) Jobs-to-be-Done, (4) Alternative analysis (real competitor isn't always who you think), (5) Product strategy including what you won't do, (6) Goals & metrics with leading vs lagging indicators, (7) Constraints & risk, (8) Synthesis into a one-pager.

**Story:** On Panini B2B my first instinct was to start with code — drafted folder structure. Stepped back, restarted with discovery. Reframed from "B2B e-commerce" to "catalog layer powering WhatsApp conversations" — hybrid model. If I'd skipped discovery I'd have built checkout-first that buyers would have ignored.

**Don't say:** "I jump in and start coding." "I follow Agile" (delivery framework, not discovery).

## Q: What is Jobs-to-be-Done?

People don't buy products, they hire products to do a job. Instead of "what features do users want?" ask "what job is the user trying to get done?"

**Story:** Instead of "what B2B features?" I asked "what job do they hire WhatsApp to do?" Answer: find designs, share with their customers, place trusted orders. So I designed the platform to do those better than WhatsApp, not replace it.

## Q: How do you handle scope creep during discovery?

Brief is the anchor. New features get evaluated against goals and out-of-scope. Serve uncommitted goals → Phase 2. Discipline isn't refusing ideas — it's deferring with respect.

**Story:** Caught myself adding multi-tenancy because "we might sell to other manufacturers." Founder pushed back — other manufacturers' requirements would diverge, we'd refactor anyway. Added to out-of-scope.

## Q: Brief vs PRD vs One-Pager?

**One-Pager/Brief:** Problem, users, strategy, metrics. 5 min read.
**PRD:** Detailed — user stories, edge cases. 30 min.
**Amazon 6-pager:** Narrative between the two.

Skipping the one-pager often means specifying solution before agreeing on problem.
